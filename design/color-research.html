A calmer colour system for Daur.

Research and working palette comparison · 18 October 2026. The selected direction is warm ivory with muted teal. This preview uses illustrative tracking data and code-drawn geometry, not a screenshot of the finished app.

The finding: the current red theme passes normal-text contrast in its main pairings. Passing contrast does not establish personal comfort. The change reduces large areas of strong colour and separates reading surfaces from action colours; it is not a medical claim that teal prevents eye strain.

Measured colour pairs

Ratios are calculated from the sRGB values using WCAG relative luminance. Normal text needs at least 4.5:1. Essential control boundaries and graphics need 3:1 against their adjacent colours where that criterion applies. These calculations do not certify the entire application.

What the research supports

1. Contrast comes before hue

WCAG defines luminance contrast, not a list of soothing colours. Measure actual text, icons, controls and states. A gentle-looking pale label can still be unreadable. W3C: text contrast; non-text contrast.

2. Use neutral surfaces and semantic accents

Material separates surface roles from primary action roles. Daur can keep its track and expressive type while replacing the full-screen red field with neutral surfaces. Teal identifies actions/progress, amber identifies attention, and red identifies errors. Material colour roles.

3. Reading performance is not the same as comfort

Buchner and Baumgartner’s proofreading experiments found better performance with dark text on light backgrounds, but did not find corresponding differences in reported fatigue or eyestrain. This supports offering a legible light appearance; it does not prove ivory or teal is universally more comfortable. Primary study, Ergonomics (2007).

4. Keep a real dark appearance

Design dark surfaces and raised surfaces separately instead of inverting every colour. Preferences, room lighting and display brightness vary, so retain system light/dark support. Validate both appearances on actual devices. Apple: Dark Mode.

Implementation decision

Selected: warm ivory #F6F3EC, raised surface #FFFDF8, muted teal #28645D, dark text #25342F. Preserve the track metaphor; place emphasis on the next action rather than the entire screen. The dark counterpart uses deep green-grey #172420 and light teal #9CCFC0.

Use clear labels, checkmarks and shapes alongside colour. Pastel teal should not carry small white text. Keep decorative dividers subtle, but essential outlines visible. Test meal logging and plan review at normal and large text sizes, in daylight and a dim room, on a real phone at a comfortable brightness.

Current light theme baseline: body text 5.82:1; secondary text 4.68:1; yellow button label 11.45:1. All pass normal-text contrast.
